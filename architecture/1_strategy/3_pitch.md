# Why this exists

_[← Strategy layer](./README.md) · [EA home](../README.md)_

A one-page brief for a sponsor. It defines nothing and names no identifiers:
every claim in it is drawn from [1_motivation.md](./1_motivation.md) and
[2_value-stream.md](./2_value-stream.md), which are where those claims are
recorded, sourced and checked. Read this one to decide; read those to argue.

---

**Most organisations already know how they work. Very few can answer a question
about it in less than a week.** What depends on this system. Who owns this
information. What breaks if we change it. The answers exist — spread across
diagrams, spreadsheets, a service desk, an asset register and the memory of
four people — and assembling them is a project every single time.

## What goes wrong today

- **The knowledge is locked in pictures.** Architecture is kept as diagrams, so
  every question is answered by a person opening files. The answer is only as
  current as the last time somebody redrew them, and it cannot be checked.
- **The facts are already somewhere else.** Most of what the architecture
  describes is owned by systems that already hold it, and it gets copied in by
  hand. It is out of date the week it is entered, and nobody can tell which
  parts are stale.
- **Nobody else can use it.** Teams, reports and AI assistants that would act on
  the architecture cannot read it. It leaves as a file somebody exports, so
  every consumer keeps a copy that is wrong the next day.
- **You cannot change what it is allowed to describe.** The vocabulary — what
  counts as a system, a process, an information asset — belongs to the tool.
  Changing it is a request to a vendor, so the model drifts from the business
  rather than following it.

## What changes

- **Answers in seconds, with their sources shown.** Ask what a change affects
  and get the answer with every fact traced back to where it came from — so it
  can be trusted, or challenged, without re-doing the work.
- **It stays current on its own.** Facts flow in from the systems that already
  own them, each one carrying where it came from and when it was last confirmed.
  Staleness becomes visible instead of invisible.
- **Everyone builds on the same answer.** The architecture sits in the data
  platform alongside everything else, so a team, a report or an AI assistant
  reads it directly. One version, no exports, no reconciling.
- **It speaks your language, and you change the language yourself.** What the
  model is allowed to describe is configuration, not a product feature. A change
  is tried on a copy and adopted when it proves out.
- **Change stays governed.** Anyone can propose; the people who own the facts
  review; a person approves. An assistant drafts and explains — it never
  approves.

## Why this is not something to buy

The products on offer each solve one half of the problem and leave the other
outside. A modelling tool holds the vocabulary and the drawings but cannot be
fed or read at scale. A data catalogue tracks where data flows but does not know
what the business does or who decides. Choosing either means building the
missing half beside it, where it belongs to nobody and is funded by no one.

## Why now

AI assistants are only as useful as what they are allowed to read. An
architecture kept as drawings is invisible to them; one kept as governed,
current, cited information is the single most valuable thing an organisation can
hand them.

## What success looks like

An impact question answered in a meeting rather than a fortnight. A steward who
can see which facts have gone stale and fix them in one pass. A team that builds
on the architecture without asking anyone for a copy. And a vocabulary that
changed three times this year because the business did.
